# Handbook sources

Web sources consulted for handbook entries (accessed 2026-11-06). These are secondary sources; averages differ by method and start date, and some include data after 2019. Used as context, never to tune on the 2020 test or 2021+ holdout.

## US midterm elections (entry 3.6b)
- Fisher Investments, S&P 500 around the past 14 midterms: https://www.fisherinvestments.com/en-us/marketminder/sp-500-performance-around-the-past-14-midterm-elections
- U.S. Bank, stock market performance after midterm elections: https://www.usbank.com/investing/financial-perspectives/market-news/stock-market-performance-after-midterm-elections.html
- Ameriprise, what to expect from markets in a midterm election year: https://www.ameriprise.com/financial-news-research/insights/2026-midterm-election-market-impacts
- Advisor Perspectives, 2018: "Midterm Elections: Gridlock Was the Best Possible Outcome": https://www.advisorperspectives.com/commentaries/2018/11/09/midterm-elections-gridlock-was-the-best-possible-outcome
- RBC Wealth Management, equities and the election effect: https://www.rbcwealthmanagement.com/en-ca/insights/equities-and-the-election-effect

Key claims taken (not copied): midterm years are weak before the vote and the following 12 months are positive in most cases since 1950; Q4 of the midterm year and the next two quarters are positive far more often than normal quarters; 2018 was an exception year for the pre-vote run-up and the post-election drift (Fed-driven Q4 slide); divided government tends to be welcomed.
